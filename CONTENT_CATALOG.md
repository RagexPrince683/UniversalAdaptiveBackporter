# UAB content catalog

UAB adapts Java Edition content through 1.21.11 to Forge 1.7.10. The native world height, loader, save format and server ownership remain in place. A modern content name does not imply complete modern engine behavior. Feature families use update-based controls in [config/uab.cfg](CONFIGURATION.md).

## Target coverage

The table and detailed behavior descriptions on this page describe the maintained 1.7.10 implementation. Forge 1.12.2 implements native materials/building variants, the retained surface/ocean/Nether biome families, modern plants and trees, aquatic content, containers and workstations. Functional content includes copper oxidation/waxing across shapes and doors, bulbs, targets, lightning rods, candles, cauldrons, dripstone, brushable blocks, decorated pots, sculk sensors/shriekers, Creaking Hearts, resin, copper chests/golems/statues and contained-water dried ghasts. The maintained living-species roster has native implementations alongside species supplied by 1.12.2; see [MOB_COVERAGE.md](MOB_COVERAGE.md) for behavioral boundaries.

Native smithing uses the recipe API, template/equipment/addition slots, cosmetic armor trims, retained equipment NBT and exact template-copying ingredients. Released models and cached audio adapt to the native client. Ocean generation retains saved shipwrecks, ruins, buried treasure, exploration-map markers and once-only loot; Nether generation retains native caves and fortresses. Saved bastions, outposts, trail ruins, Ancient Cities and Trial Chambers include their maintained loot, finite encounters and vaults; native woodland mansions remain native. Music discs use native jukebox insertion, playback, loot and comparator values. Ten boat materials include chest boats and bamboo rafts using native passenger, movement and inventory packets; source item/vehicle identities remain available alongside native boats. Eleven wood-sign variants use native sign text, placement and editing packets. Undyed shulker boxes reuse native inventory/opening behavior, while storage bans, dyeing/washing and tooltip controls also apply at native boundaries. Client support includes native lighting/gamma/night vision, End flashes, Nether ambience/particles, bounded breaking particles, later note instruments, sound controls, resource overrides, game-mode selection and progress-driven loading screens. Native flower pots accept and render the maintained additional plants. Gameplay adapters retain item flotation/fire resistance, ascending Elytra takeoff, horse/jump-climbing rules, sleeping percentages, damage direction, Nether sponge drying and block-inventory extension points. Optional native integrations include metadata-preserving modded raw/deepslate ores, Baubles Mending and Iron Chests storage upgrades. Upgraded barrels and shulker boxes keep one authoritative inventory, retained tier metadata, larger native containers and maintained tier skins. Rose/old-gravel recipes and the legacy daylight-detector identity remain available. Alternate husk/stray spawning and optional natural End shulkers retain their controls. General waterlogging and a swimming engine remain outside the maintained contained-water adaptations. Implementation coverage does not establish complete gameplay acceptance for every optional mod combination.

Feature metadata distinguishes **native** target functionality, families requiring a **full backport**, and **adapted** requirements such as mixed families and custom rules. These classifications describe what the platform lacks; they are independent of implementation or runtime acceptance. Component dates prevent later additions from being hidden just because the base family already exists in vanilla. Custom recipes, mod integrations and alternate gameplay rules belong to General.

## Adaptation boundaries

Read [mob support](MOB_COVERAGE.md) and [world generation](WORLD_GENERATION.md) for specific behaviors, dependencies and saved-state rules. Modern surface/ocean biomes use native climate selection. Structures use released templates translated into saved, chunk-bounded legacy assembly. Existing terrain is not regenerated.

There is no general waterlogging/swimming engine, world-height or global cave-engine replacement, modern combat rewrite, full modern village-profession system. Fletching tables are decorative and legacy dragon combat remains. Catalyst XP spreading stays unfinished and disabled. Missing ingredients or reward prerequisites are deferred rather than replaced with unrelated content.

UAB retains technical `etfuturum` identities, entity numbers, resource domains, network channel and saved NBT/structure names. Disabling acquisition does not remove those identities or erase existing content. Optional integrations use the other mod's actual installed API/content and do not replace UAB's canonical systems.

## Ocean plants

Kelp (`etfuturum:kelp`) grows in source water on supported seabeds. Natural random-tick growth and bone meal extend a column up to 26 blocks; this native adaptation uses a height limit rather than the modern 25-age tip state. Breaking support drops the detached segments and restores their water. Smelting or smoking kelp produces `etfuturum:dried_kelp`, a fast-eaten food. Nine dried kelp pack into `etfuturum:dried_kelp_block`, which unpacks into nine and fuels 20 furnace operations.

Sea pickles (`etfuturum:sea_pickle`) stack into clusters of one to four. Submerged clusters emit light levels 6/9/12/15, while dry clusters emit no light. Bucket interactions fill/drain their contained source water. The internal `etfuturum:sea_pickle_underwater` block drops the same sea-pickle item; cluster state never becomes item damage. Smelting a sea pickle produces lime dye. Bone meal fills a submerged cluster and spreads nearby pickles only over living coral blocks.

The **Update Aquatic** kelp, sea-pickle, ocean-plant and coral-reef generation controls govern new acquisition independently of fish spawning. New Overworld ocean chunks receive kelp outside warm/frozen oceans and occasional sea-pickle clusters in warm oceans. Warm oceans also receive bounded tree, shelf and branching coral formations. Existing chunks are not regenerated. These blocks supply their own contained-water behavior without installing a general fluidlogging engine.

Tube, brain, bubble, fire and horn coral each have living/dead blocks, plants, floor fans and wall fans. Living coral dies after a short delay without contained or adjacent water. Ordinary coral-block harvesting yields dead coral; Silk Touch preserves the living block. Plants and fans require Silk Touch. Dry and submerged plant/fan identities preserve correct native materials and always return the canonical dry item. Dead coral cannot be revived by refilling it.

## Conduits and ocean structures

Conduits craft from eight nautilus shells around a Heart of the Sea. A submerged conduit requires a water-filled 3x3x3 interior and at least 16 prismarine/sea-lantern frame blocks in the three intersecting 5x5 rings. Range increases from 32 to 96 blocks as the frame fills. Wet players receive native Water Breathing, Night Vision and Haste for 13 seconds, refreshed every two seconds; a complete 42-block frame also attacks one wet hostile within eight blocks for four magic damage. This adaptation uses native potion effects and a retained static model rather than a separate Conduit Power effect and the modern animated eye/cage renderer.

Buried treasure generates on suitable new beaches and supplies a Heart of the Sea when conduits are enabled. Shipwrecks use released upright, sideways, inverted, full/partial and degraded layouts. Ocean ruins use released warm and cold pieces, including cold masonry overlays, native loot and drowned. Saved plans index each affected chunk, admit only loaded new footprints, and place each chunk once; existing terrain and consumed loot are retained. Conservative admission can skip a structure when its neighboring footprint was populated first. Dolphin feeding prefers nearby saved treasure/shipwrecks/ruins, retaining the monument fallback and never generating terrain to find a target.

Ocean loot uses released tables with native fish metadata, items and enchantment pools. Unsupported exploration-map markers, unavailable armor trims and other missing rewards are deferred. Stair/slab/door waterlogging, exact modern reef/ruin clustering algorithms, swimming poses and general fluid physics remain separate engine work.

## Archaeology, pottery and smithing

Brush suspicious sand and gravel by holding use on the block. Ten strokes reveal its server-selected reward and leave normal sand or gravel; damage is charged to the brush only when an excavation finishes. Interrupted progress decays. Breaking a suspicious block, including with Silk Touch, destroys its reward. Unsupported suspicious blocks fall and break on landing. Progress and the selected reward survive saving. New desert pyramids retain their native TNT/treasure chamber and gain a buried excavation pocket; desert wells, warm/cold ocean ruins and buried trail ruins supply their own released archaeology loot tables. Existing terrain receives no retrogen. Trail ruins can supply Relic and Wayfinder/Shaper/Raiser/Host templates. Unsupported common decoration rewards (colored candles and hanging signs) remain deferred; available dyes, glass panes and other native rewards keep their actual identities. Trial Chamber pots retain their released sherd designs, including Flow/Guster/Scrape.

All 23 pottery sherds are separate items. A three-by-three crafting grid uses one brick or sherd above, left, right and below its empty center, storing back/left/right/front independently. Placed pots and pot items render those designs. Pots accept one stack through use or hoppers and report fullness to comparators. Hand harvesting or Silk Touch retains the decorated pot; ordinary tools, arrows, thrown projectiles and explosions shatter it into its four ingredients. Stored items drop separately. Older dispenser-backed pots migrate without losing any of their saved stacks; excess legacy stacks remain saved until the pot is broken. General pot waterlogging and insertion wobble are not implemented.

The Smithing Table has template, equipment and addition slots. Built-in netherite upgrades consume a Netherite Upgrade template, equipment and ingot. The **Trails & Tales / Netherite upgrade templates** setting can retain the earlier two-input progression. Existing CraftTweaker two-input recipes still work with an empty template slot. Names, damage, enchantments, trims and other equipment NBT survive upgrades. Applying a trim consumes a trim template and material while retaining the armor data; applying the identical trim again produces no result. Trims are cosmetic, with worn-armor overlays, inventory marks and tooltips. Armor that supplies a non-biped custom renderer may need an integration to display its worn trim.

All 18 patterns and eleven materials (including resin) are supported, subject to their content settings. Seven diamonds, an existing template and its released substrate duplicate a template into two. No unrelated substitute is used if the substrate is disabled. Coast comes from shipwrecks and Tide from elder guardians; Dune/Wild/Eye/Rib/Sentry/Spire/Snout/Vex retain their corresponding temple, stronghold, fortress, outpost, End City, bastion and mansion sources. Ancient Cities and vaults supply Ward/Silence and Flow/Bolt. Netherite upgrade templates come from bastions, guaranteed in treasure chests. These independent template rolls leave the existing legacy treasure pools intact.

## Release attribution

Minor versions are distinct where content actually differs. Salmon-size variants date to [Java 1.21.2](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-2), while the salmon mob itself dates to 1.13. Smooth named blocks begin in 1.13; their smelting recipes and additional shapes arrive in [Village & Pillage](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/village---pillage-out-java-). Mojang's [1.21.7 release notes](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-7) identify Lava Chicken; its [1.21.9 release notes](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-9) identify End light flashes. The ceiling remains 1.21.11. Historical audit records describe their own validation checkpoints and are not a blanket acceptance claim for this release.
