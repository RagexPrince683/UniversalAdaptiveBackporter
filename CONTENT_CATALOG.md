# UAB content catalog

UAB adapts Java Edition content through 1.21.11 to Forge 1.7.10. The native world height, loader, save format and server ownership remain in place. A modern content name does not imply complete modern engine behavior. Feature families use update-based controls in [config/uab.cfg](CONFIGURATION.md).

## Target coverage

The table and detailed behavior descriptions on this page describe the maintained 1.7.10 implementation. Forge 1.12.2 implements native materials/building variants, the retained surface/ocean/Nether biome families, modern plants and trees, aquatic content, containers and workstations. Functional content includes copper oxidation/waxing across shapes and doors, bulbs, targets, lightning rods, candles, cauldrons, dripstone, brushable blocks, decorated pots, sculk sensors/shriekers, Creaking Hearts, resin, copper chests/golems/statues and contained-water dried ghasts. The maintained living-species roster has native implementations alongside species supplied by 1.12.2; see [MOB_COVERAGE.md](MOB_COVERAGE.md) for behavioral boundaries.

Native smithing uses the recipe API, template/equipment/addition slots, cosmetic armor trims, retained equipment NBT and exact template-copying ingredients. Released models and cached audio adapt to the native client. Ocean generation retains saved shipwrecks, ruins, buried treasure, exploration-map markers and once-only loot; Nether generation retains native caves and fortresses. Saved bastions, outposts, trail ruins, Ancient Cities and Trial Chambers include their maintained loot, finite encounters and vaults; native woodland mansions remain native. Music discs use native jukebox insertion, playback, loot and comparator values. Ten boat materials include chest boats and bamboo rafts using native passenger, movement and inventory packets; source item/vehicle identities remain available alongside native boats. Eleven wood-sign variants use native sign text, placement and editing packets. Undyed shulker boxes reuse native inventory/opening behavior, while storage bans, dyeing/washing and tooltip controls also apply at native boundaries. Client support includes native lighting/gamma/night vision, End flashes, Nether ambience/particles, bounded breaking particles, later note instruments, sound controls, resource overrides, game-mode selection and progress-driven loading screens. Native flower pots accept and render the maintained additional plants. Gameplay adapters retain item flotation/fire resistance, ascending Elytra takeoff, horse/jump-climbing rules, sleeping percentages, damage direction, Nether sponge drying and block-inventory extension points. Optional native integrations include metadata-preserving modded raw/deepslate ores, Baubles Mending and Iron Chests storage upgrades. Upgraded barrels and shulker boxes keep one authoritative inventory, retained tier metadata, larger native containers and maintained tier skins. Rose/old-gravel recipes and the legacy daylight-detector identity remain available. Alternate husk/stray spawning and optional natural End shulkers retain their controls. General waterlogging and a swimming engine remain outside the maintained contained-water adaptations. Implementation coverage does not establish complete gameplay acceptance for every optional mod combination.

Feature metadata distinguishes **native** target functionality, families requiring a **full backport**, and **adapted** requirements such as mixed families and custom rules. These classifications describe what the platform lacks; they are independent of implementation or runtime acceptance. Component dates prevent later additions from being hidden just because the base family already exists in vanilla. Custom recipes, mod integrations and alternate gameplay rules belong to General.

## Adaptation boundaries

Read [mob support](MOB_COVERAGE.md) and [world generation](WORLD_GENERATION.md) for specific behaviors, dependencies and saved-state rules. Modern surface/ocean biomes use native climate selection. Structures use released templates translated into saved, chunk-bounded legacy assembly. Existing terrain is not regenerated.

There is no general waterlogging/swimming engine, world-height or global cave-engine replacement, modern combat rewrite, full modern village-profession system. Fletching tables are decorative and legacy dragon combat remains. Catalysts adapt native mob XP into saved, bounded spreading charge. Missing ingredients or reward prerequisites are deferred rather than replaced with unrelated content.

UAB retains technical `etfuturum` identities, entity numbers, resource domains, network channel and saved NBT/structure names. Disabling acquisition does not remove those identities or erase existing content. Optional integrations use the other mod's actual installed API/content and do not replace UAB's canonical systems.

## Ocean plants

Kelp (`etfuturum:kelp`) grows in source water on supported seabeds. Natural random-tick growth and bone meal extend a column up to 26 blocks; this native adaptation uses a height limit rather than the modern 25-age tip state. Breaking support drops the detached segments and restores their water. Smelting or smoking kelp produces `etfuturum:dried_kelp`, a fast-eaten food. Nine dried kelp pack into `etfuturum:dried_kelp_block`, which unpacks into nine and fuels 20 furnace operations.

Sea pickles (`etfuturum:sea_pickle`) stack into clusters of one to four. Submerged clusters emit light levels 6/9/12/15, while dry clusters emit no light. Bucket interactions fill/drain their contained source water. The internal `etfuturum:sea_pickle_underwater` block drops the same sea-pickle item; cluster state never becomes item damage. Smelting a sea pickle produces lime dye. Bone meal fills a submerged cluster and spreads nearby pickles only over living coral blocks.

The **Update Aquatic** kelp, sea-pickle, ocean-plant and coral-reef generation controls govern new acquisition independently of fish spawning. New Overworld ocean chunks receive kelp outside warm/frozen oceans and occasional sea-pickle clusters in warm oceans. Warm oceans also receive bounded tree, shelf and branching coral formations. Existing chunks are not regenerated. These blocks supply their own contained-water behavior without installing a general fluidlogging engine.

Tube, brain, bubble, fire and horn coral each have living/dead blocks, plants, floor fans and wall fans. Living coral dies after a short delay without contained or adjacent water. Ordinary coral-block harvesting yields dead coral; Silk Touch preserves the living block. Plants and fans require Silk Touch. Dry and submerged plant/fan identities preserve correct native materials and always return the canonical dry item. Dead coral cannot be revived by refilling it.

## Conduits and ocean structures

Conduits craft from eight nautilus shells around a Heart of the Sea. A submerged conduit requires a water-filled 3x3x3 interior and at least 16 prismarine/sea-lantern frame blocks in the three intersecting 5x5 rings. Range increases from 32 to 96 blocks as the frame fills. Wet players receive native Water Breathing, Night Vision and Haste for 13 seconds, refreshed every two seconds; a complete 42-block frame also attacks one wet hostile within eight blocks for four magic damage. This adaptation uses native potion effects and a retained static model rather than a separate Conduit Power effect and the modern animated eye/cage renderer.

Buried treasure generates on suitable new beaches and supplies a Heart of the Sea when conduits are enabled. Shipwrecks use released upright, sideways, inverted, full/partial and degraded layouts. Ocean ruins use released warm and cold pieces, including cold masonry overlays, native loot and drowned. Saved plans index each affected chunk, admit only loaded new footprints, and place each chunk once; existing terrain and consumed loot are retained. Conservative admission can skip a structure when its neighboring footprint was populated first. Dolphin feeding prefers nearby saved treasure/shipwrecks/ruins, retaining the monument fallback and never generating terrain to find a target.

Ocean loot uses released tables with native fish metadata, items and enchantment pools. Unsupported exploration-map markers, unavailable armor trims and other missing rewards are deferred. Stair/slab/door waterlogging, exact modern reef/ruin clustering algorithms, swimming poses and general fluid physics remain separate engine work.

## Archaeology, pottery and smithing

Brush suspicious sand and gravel by holding use on the block. Ten strokes reveal its server-selected reward and leave normal sand or gravel; damage is charged to the brush only when an excavation finishes. Interrupted progress decays. Breaking a suspicious block, including with Silk Touch, destroys its reward. Unsupported suspicious blocks fall and break on landing. Progress and the selected reward survive saving. New desert pyramids retain their native TNT/treasure chamber and gain a buried excavation pocket; desert wells, warm/cold ocean ruins and buried trail ruins supply their own released archaeology loot tables. Existing terrain receives no retrogen. Trail ruins can supply Relic and Wayfinder/Shaper/Raiser/Host templates. Unavailable colored-candle rewards remain deferred; enabled hanging-sign families now resolve normally; available dyes, glass panes and other native rewards keep their actual identities. Trial Chamber pots retain their released sherd designs, including Flow/Guster/Scrape.

All 23 pottery sherds are separate items. A three-by-three crafting grid uses one brick or sherd above, left, right and below its empty center, storing back/left/right/front independently. Placed pots and pot items render those designs. Pots accept one stack through use or hoppers and report fullness to comparators. Hand harvesting or Silk Touch retains the decorated pot; ordinary tools, arrows, thrown projectiles and explosions shatter it into its four ingredients. Stored items drop separately. Older dispenser-backed pots migrate without losing any of their saved stacks; excess legacy stacks remain saved until the pot is broken. General pot waterlogging and insertion wobble are not implemented.

The Smithing Table has template, equipment and addition slots. Built-in netherite upgrades consume a Netherite Upgrade template, equipment and ingot. The **Trails & Tales / Netherite upgrade templates** setting can retain the earlier two-input progression. Existing CraftTweaker two-input recipes still work with an empty template slot. Names, damage, enchantments, trims and other equipment NBT survive upgrades. Applying a trim consumes a trim template and material while retaining the armor data; applying the identical trim again produces no result. Trims are cosmetic, with worn-armor overlays, inventory marks and tooltips. Armor that supplies a non-biped custom renderer may need an integration to display its worn trim.

All 18 patterns and eleven materials (including resin) are supported, subject to their content settings. Seven diamonds, an existing template and its released substrate duplicate a template into two. No unrelated substitute is used if the substrate is disabled. Coast comes from shipwrecks and Tide from elder guardians; Dune/Wild/Eye/Rib/Sentry/Spire/Snout/Vex retain their corresponding temple, stronghold, fortress, outpost, End City, bastion and mansion sources. Ancient Cities and vaults supply Ward/Silence and Flow/Bolt. Netherite upgrade templates come from bastions, guaranteed in treasure chests. These independent template rolls leave the existing legacy treasure pools intact.

## Release attribution

Minor versions are distinct where content actually differs. Salmon-size variants date to [Java 1.21.2](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-2), while the salmon mob itself dates to 1.13. Smooth named blocks begin in 1.13; their smelting recipes and additional shapes arrive in [Village & Pillage](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/village---pillage-out-java-). Mojang's [1.21.7 release notes](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-7) identify Lava Chicken; its [1.21.9 release notes](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-java-edition-1-21-9) identify End light flashes. The ceiling remains 1.21.11. Historical audit records describe their own validation checkpoints and are not a blanket acceptance claim for this release.

## Completion audit and survival additions

The [modern-content implementation matrix](MODERN_CONTENT_AUDIT.md) records confirmed behavior, remaining gaps, dependencies and priorities across both targets. UAB is not a complete implementation of every feature through 1.21.11.

Campfires and soul campfires accept four independently cooking native food inputs, finishing after 30 seconds without fuel. Progress and inputs persist. Shovels or water extinguish them; flint and steel or fire charges relight dry campfires. Lit campfires calm bees through the retained smoke checks. Their released fire/log models and native smoke are used; food inputs render and synchronize to clients, hay bales extend signal smoke, Frost Walker protects against heat, burning arrows relight dry fires, and buckets remove locally contained water.

Honeycomb and honey bottles are harvested from full nests/hives with shears/bottles, including through dispensers. Honeycomb Blocks use four honeycomb; Honey Blocks use four honey bottles, returning their glass containers. A Honey Block plus four glass bottles returns four honey bottles. Honey bottles can also craft sugar. Copper continues to use the existing honeycomb/wax and axe paths. Native 1.12.2 honey piston movement now retains adhesion, slime separation and entity carrying.

On 1.7.10, parrots and igloos have independent update-organized controls. The Elder Guardian egg has a distinct entity identity while older Guardian entities with saved Elder NBT remain valid. Native 1.12.2 parrots, igloos, elders and stronghold distribution are retained.

Maces use a Heavy Core above a Breeze Rod, retain 500 durability and repair with Breeze Rods. Falling more than 1.5 blocks supplies the released smash-damage curve and nearby impact knockback; only a successful hit resets vertical momentum and establishes the saved fall-damage baseline. Density increases smash damage, Breach reduces actual armor effectiveness, and Wind Burst launches a non-destructive impulse. Native combat cadence is retained on 1.7.10; 1.12.2 uses the weapon's native attack-speed attribute. Wind Burst comes from ominous-vault enchanted books, rather than ordinary table/trade rolls. Native particles adapt the modern gust presentation.

## Adaptive content behavior

Grindstones repair matching equipment, remove removable enchantments and award experience when the server commits output extraction. Native curses are retained; the legacy adapter also retains unknown or named-curse entries. Existing workstation handling covers cursor, shift, hotbar and drop actions.

Chiseled bookshelves hold six books, written/writable books or enchanted books. Click a front slot to insert or remove one; comparators report the last accessed slot. Shelves hold three item stacks and swap a clicked slot with the main hand. Power a row of up to three matching-facing shelves to swap three, six or nine rightmost hotbar slots; longer rows stay individually operable. Contents, comparator history and visual updates persist and synchronize.

Bells ring by hand, projectiles or rising redstone. They alert native villagers toward their existing homes and identify nearby raiders using native glowing where available or a legacy particle cue. Hanging signs use native four-line editing and ownership checks, with ceiling and wall placement for enabled wood families. Spyglasses use held right-click, first-person zoom and a scope overlay.

Spring to Life adds leaf litter, wildflowers, bushes, firefly bushes, cactus flowers, short/tall dry grass, fallen logs and leaf particles. Layered plants stack; appropriate plants support bonemeal, shears, dyes, fuel and composting. Native biomes guide decoration and animal appearance. Cow/pig/chicken coats, nine wolf coats, seven wolf voices and climate sheep colors retain native AI. Brown/blue eggs work in food recipes and hatch the corresponding chicken type. Breeding preserves a parent's variant; saved older animals keep their existing temperate appearance.

Copper gear uses native mining, armor and enchanting systems, repairs with copper and recycles into nuggets. Copper horse armor uses native horse storage and tracking, with an additional crafting route. Aging fixtures reuse the existing wax/axe/lightning behavior while retaining orientation, inventories and rod power. Adapted behavior deliberately preserves the legacy engine and compatibility; exact upstream rates, restrictions, loot and presentation are not completeness requirements.

Copper chests retain their native 27-slot inventories across oxidation, waxing and scraping. Their eight weather/wax identities share the existing copper-chest setting and golem sorting integration. The single-chest inventory remains the deliberate legacy adaptation.

Calibrated sculk sensors use their back redstone input as a frequency filter, detect within sixteen blocks, and expose the received frequency to comparators. Native events feed a chunk-indexed listener set with wool occlusion and persisted pulses. Catalysts retain bounded spreading charge from awarded mob XP; each successful sculk conversion spends one charge and excess capacity remains native XP. These are native-engine adaptations.

Lodestones bind ordinary compasses to block positions in the current dimension. Binding consumes one compass, keeps its custom name and tags, and supports retargeting. Destroyed targets invalidate when their chunks are loaded; pointing never requests distant chunks. Recovery compasses craft from a compass surrounded by eight echo shards from Ancient Cities. A compass in a player inventory follows that holder's last recorded death, survives native respawn persistence, and spins when no death is known or its dimension differs. Worlds predating this feature acquire a target on the next death. Native compass frames adapt needle presentation without replacing vanilla compass behavior. Copper horse armor uses native horse geometry and UV coverage with the released copper palette.

Spring animal texture overrides are **off by default** on both targets. Opt in with the client setting `update_1_21_5.animal_appearances.enabled=true` to use UAB cow, pig, chicken and wolf textures. Leaving it off preserves vanilla and other mods' animal appearances, including custom models. This setting does not disable Spring to Life breeding, egg variants, saved animal data or other gameplay. UAB does not mutate shared vanilla model UVs.

Lecterns craft from four wooden slabs and a bookshelf. Insert a writable or signed book to read it, turn synchronized pages and produce comparator output from 1 to 15. Page changes pulse redstone for two ticks; removing the book clears both outputs. Books and pages persist, and hoppers below can extract the book. The adapted read-only screen preserves book contents and formatting, supports scrolling long pages and requires inventory space to take the book. Lecterns do not take ownership of existing village professions.

Rooted dirt is obtainable from growing azaleas into trees and from new wandering traders. Bone meal on rooted dirt grows hanging roots into an empty space below it; using a hoe restores dirt and releases a hanging-root item. Roots attach to solid ceilings, require shears to collect when placed, and can be composted. Existing tree-generation hooks remain authoritative; this adds no cave-biome generation.

The 1.7.10 shield crafts from six planks and an iron ingot. Hold use to raise it; protection begins after five ticks and follows the direction of the incoming legacy damage source. Strong blocked attacks wear its 336-point durability, planks repair it in an anvil, and axe attacks can disable all shields held by that player for five seconds. The native blocking pose adapts its use to the older single-hand inventory. Existing swords retain their own blocking rules; 1.12.2 retains vanilla shields.

Lunge I–III can enchant spears through native enchanting, books/anvils and supported released loot. A primary attack or empty-air jab supplies a horizontal movement impulse, costs one additional durability and adds exhaustion when at least three hunger icons remain. It cannot activate while riding, underwater or gliding. Its separate one-second movement limit leaves each target’s attack timing, reach and damage rules intact. No full combat overhaul is supplied.

Vanilla firework rockets boost elytra flight on both targets, with flight-duration 1–3 rockets in the UAB item tab. The legacy adaptation supports air and block clicks while gliding, respects canceled interactions and consumes a rocket only after its boosting entity is accepted. The 1.12.2 target retains native firework boosting and crafting.

Netherite horse armor upgrades diamond horse armor with a netherite ingot at the smithing table, using an upgrade template when that setting is enabled. It preserves the base item’s saved data and uses native horse equipment, protection and model geometry on each target.

Crafters provide a native three-by-three recipe grid, disabled slots, comparator output, hopper insertion/extraction and six output directions. A rising redstone pulse crafts once after a short delay. Crafted items and recipe containers share saved delivery ownership; interrupted or refused delivery retains only the unaccepted remainder. Minecraft 1.7.10 adapts recipe remainders through Forge container-item callbacks, while 1.12.2 uses each recipe’s remaining-items API and native item-handler integration.

Bundles provide 64 units of weighted storage, native inventory right-click interactions, last-in-first-out retrieval, capacity bars, contents tooltips and all sixteen dye colors. Nested bundles reserve additional space, and shulker boxes are excluded. Recoloring preserves saved contents and item data. The native inventory adaptation leaves unrelated clicks and slot permissions intact; crafting previews cannot be edited as owned containers. Bundle crafting uses string above leather.

Scaffolding crafts six blocks from six bamboo and one string. Support travels down columns and up to six blocks horizontally. Unsupported new blocks fall through native falling-block entities; losing an established support releases the affected scaffold blocks. Native one-way platforms allow jumping upward and sneaking downward. Placement checks, item consumption and world save ownership use each target's native APIs.

Powder snow uses local collision and saved entity freezing state. Prolonged exposure slows and damages unprotected living entities; leather armor prevents further freezing, and leaving the snow thaws accumulated cold. Native cold-adapted mobs are immune. Ordinary unmounted, unleashed skeletons can convert into strays while retaining their saved equipment and name. Snowfall collection reuses native weather samples, and dispensers can place or collect powder snow with buckets. No mountain terrain, general waterlogging or global movement replacement is added.

Respawn anchors craft from six crying obsidian and three glowstone blocks. They provide charge-dependent light and comparator output, saved player bindings, local safe-exit selection and native multiplayer respawns. A validated death respawn scopes the Nether-provider exception to the native player-respawn call; dimension providers and their generation stay in place. Native spawn-point changes remain authoritative, End returns keep their normal path, and charges are consumed only after the player actually respawns at the anchor.
